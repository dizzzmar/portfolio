# Design detail: Plant Lab, Playground and the dot field

> **Playground is hidden for now (Nov 2026):** `/playground/`, `/playground/island/` and `/playground/plant-lab/` still exist and work by their address, but nothing links to them any more (no menu entry, no terminal command, no sitemap, `llms.txt`, `machine/` or prefetch) and they carry `noindex`. To show it again: put back a «Playground» item in `js/menu.js`, remove the `noindex` line from the three pages, add them to `sitemap.xml`, `llms.txt` and `machine/index.html`, and add `"/playground/"` to the prefetch list of each page. The Plant Lab project page (`/project/plant-lab/`) is separate and public; its «full screen» button still goes to `/playground/plant-lab/`.

Detail for this area of the design system. `DESIGN.md` holds the system and a summary; read this file only when working on this area, and keep new per-page detail here, not in `DESIGN.md`.

### The Playground menu (/playground/)
On a phone (up to 760 px) it uses the same layout as the Digital Archive index (`css/archive.css`; see `.claude/notes/design/archive.md`): the title «Playground» at the top, on one line (`archive-heading--word`, 18.5 vw, 110 px max, because it is one long word) and the entries «Island» and «Plant Lab» at the bottom, large, at thumb height. The page stays hidden (no links, `noindex`).

The Playground is a menu of small experiments with **the same design as the Digital Archive index** (it uses `css/archive.css` and the pixel trail of `js/archive-trail.js`; see `.claude/notes/design/archive.md`): page black with cream text, the title «Playground» in a left column and one row per experiment in the right one (the same two-column structure as «Selected projects» on the landing page), with no subtitle, no count and no lines between rows. There are two entries, **Island** and **Plant Lab**, which opens the full-screen sandbox. To add an entry, copy a `<li>` of `playground/index.html` and create its folder in `playground/`.

### Plant Lab and the Playground sandbox (/project/plant-lab/, /playground/plant-lab/)
A node sandbox in the look of a Belgian typographic poster (the reference: two columns, times with «+» bullets, small capitals next to huge numerals, black and white): a black field and white sheets. It exists twice with the same code (js/playground/, css/sandbox.css): inside the Plant Lab project page, and full screen at /playground/plant-lab/ (black page, with a second group: a test shape with its own sheet; its running title «Plant Lab» goes back to the menu at /playground/; it was at /playground/ until the menu was added).
- **Plant Lab page:** a project page like the others (running title, sticky left column with Year, Client «Personal», Discipline «Procedural drawing, creative code» and four short paragraphs, "More works" at the end). The copy stresses that the human quality of the strokes (hand tremor, fading pressure, pencil and paper grain, the handwriting of the names) is generated procedurally, with no image, scan, texture or font behind the drawings. The right column holds one piece: the sandbox, a black block with soft corners, 80 % of the screen high (64 % on a phone). It is listed in Work under «Personal», after Posters, with eight cut-outs of generated plants.
- **Nodes are sheets of paper, not boxes:** white, with large equal corners (40 px, `--sb-radius`; the black frame of the project page 48 px), a title in Funnel Display at 36 px (`--fs-node`), input ports and output ports in small capitals on the left and right, and the settings as a list: each line a «+» and its name in small capitals, the value in lower case at the right. The Seed node is its number at 64 px (`--fs-numeral`), the Sheet node carries the seed at 36 px in its title and the drawing's name in capitals under the paper, like the list of names at the foot of the poster. New nodes grow in (scale .92 → 1). The paper of every drawing has soft corners too. The only colours on the field are white, black and the drawings' paper.
- **Wires and ports:** wires are strings of white beads (3 px dots every 8 px), ports are white drops with a black ring that sit across the sheet's edge and fill in when linked. Only ports of the same type connect (seed, traits, pose, brush, ink, paper, drawing) and cycles are refused. Connect by dragging, by tapping a port and then another, or with the keyboard (Enter on a port, then on another; Delete on a port removes its wires). Dragging from a connected input detaches the wire; dropping it on empty canvas removes it. Each wire has a small × (shown on hover, always on touch) 36 px before its input port.
- **The editor:** a canvas with a faint dot grid that pans (drag the background or any part of a sheet that is not a control; middle mouse button anywhere), zooms (Ctrl/Cmd + wheel, pinch, − and +) and fits (Fit). The page wheel keeps scrolling the page: the sandbox never traps it. Add node (menu, a white sheet too), Fit and Reset (and, in the project page, Full screen) are opaque pills at the bottom left of the frame; − and + at the bottom right. On a phone, in the project page one finger that starts inside the sandbox keeps scrolling the page (vertical) and the canvas only pans sideways; Full screen is where it moves freely. On touch, port rows are spaced so each port has its own 38 px finger area, a finger that starts on a port never scrolls the page, a tap that trembles up to 12 px still counts as a tap, and a gesture the system cancels drops the wire instead of connecting it where the finger stopped; Esc clears a selected port. Limits, on purpose: nodes cannot be moved with the keyboard (their controls and ports can all be reached and used), and the second sheet in /playground/ starts with the default brush, ink and paper so there are no long wires crossing the graph.
- **Nodes** (the original Plant Lab and Brush Lab code, only the way of handling it changed): Seed, Pose, Ink, Paper, Medium, Stroke / Grain / Hand (gains that multiply the brush they receive, so they work on any medium), Structure / Foliage / Flower / Treatment (chained trait locks; each select shows «Random» or the value you fixed, in bold), Plant (shows the species name and its traits), Study (a sheet of specimens), Traits (every variant of one trait), Shape (test shapes, optional compare of the six media) and Sheet (draws what it receives with the connected brush, ink and paper; Save PNG). What is not connected falls back to a default, so a loose Sheet still draws. No status bars, scores or hints: the only messages are «Heavy drawing…» and the empty-sheet label for screen readers. The starting layout stacks each column by the measured height of its nodes.
- **Optical padding:** inside every sheet the ink (not the box) sits 28 px from the four edges (`--sb-pad`), measured on a capture of each node: the last element of each node brings its own inner air (a text line, a slider, a button), so the bottom padding is discounted per node type (`--sb-pb`). Selected rings (ink dots, media) hang into the margin; the objects themselves align with the text.
- **Legibility:** the sandbox opens at zoom 1 on the plant → sheet chain, so the small capitals (11 px) are read at 11 px; below zoom 0.8 they fall under 9 px, so Fit (the overview) is a choice, never the start. Overlapping sheets are cut apart by a 4 px black outline.
- **Controls:** the one filled pill is Random; media are their own stroke (chosen one bold and framed); sliders are a 2 px line with a 16 px dot; selects show their value over the real `<select>`.
- **Engine:** engine.js (seeded noise, the dot brush, paper), plant-engine.js (plant geometry, traits, names, handwriting), render.js (draws a sheet from a drawing, a brush, an ink and a paper, without page state), brush-shapes.js, lab-kit.js (sliders and pickers), editor.js (the generic node editor), nodes.js (the node types), sandbox.js (the starting graph and layout) and mount.js. Nothing is uploaded: everything is drawn in the browser. Seeds are random on opening.
- **Motion:** the sandbox rises 18 px when the page opens (`pgn-in`); new nodes grow in; sliders, buttons and ports give a quick press; with reduced motion none of it.
- **Dot field background (Playground only; Posters and the Digital Archive use the pixel trail instead):** a field of tiny dots (0.5 px radius, 11 px gaps) sits behind the page, from React Bits «DotField» ported to plain JavaScript (js/dot-field.js, mounted by js/dot-bg.js through `data-dot-bg` on `<body>`). A lens follows the cursor and magnifies the dots under it (it deepens while pressing), a slow wave rolls through the field and a few dots twinkle; it pulls into focus on arrival. White dots on the dark pages (`data-dot-bg="white"`, base opacity 0.45) and dark ink dots on Posters (`data-dot-bg="ink"`, the light version, base opacity 0.4). One fixed full-screen canvas behind the content that never catches clicks; with reduced motion the field is still; no WebGL2 or data saver: no field. On the Playground it replaces the node editor's own fixed dot grid (so the grid no longer pans and zooms with the canvas; the editor background is transparent and the page colour shows through). The values live at the top of js/dot-bg.js.

### Island (/playground/island/)
The pixel-art isometric world that used to be the back of the About portrait (the portrait no longer flips). It is a full-screen page like Plant Lab: running title «Island» (links back to /playground/) beside the tomato, the scene filling the window (`js/island.js` mounts `js/about-scene.js` in its full-screen mode, one canvas inside `.island-stage`), and the controls as pills (`css/island.css`): time of day (Morning, Day, Sunset, Night, Now) and weather (Clear, Cloudy, Rain, Auto) at the bottom left, zoom − and + at the bottom right (hidden on a phone, where you pinch). The wheel or a pinch zooms (also + and −, double click or double tap), dragging pans (also the arrow keys). There is no Close pill: the title goes back. **Seeing through the foliage:** where a tree, bush or cloud of leaves is in front of a person, it turns into a sparse dither of pixels (about one pixel in five kept at the centre, fading back to solid at the edge), like the vegetation in Zelda: Breath of the Wild; at the campfire, the picnic, the whiteboard, the hammock and the swing the cleared area also covers what they are doing (the fire, the blanket…). Done in the foliage shader with `SEE` (one entry per person, updated every frame). **Walking:** people only change level by the stairs, the bridges and the rope bridge (the rope bridge now reaches into the islet, which was cut off); they get up from a seat towards its front and leave from there; if a place cannot be reached on foot they do something else instead of walking straight through things. **Phase 2 (Nov 2026): one finer grain and pixel-art people.** Every scene pixel is about 2.7 cm of the world on any screen (`WIDE_FULL` 1300, textures at `DENS` 36 per metre; it used to be 4–5 cm and 2 × 2 screen pixels on a computer, so the detail was lost), and full screen shows the whole island a little smaller, with air around it. Leaf clusters are drawn at about 8 pixels (one pixel of their drawing = one scene pixel; `LEAF_K` scales their size and multiplies their number to cover the same). **People** are drawn like Stardew Valley characters (`pixelPerson`, `drawPeople`): the box skeleton still moves with every pose and casts the shadow but is invisible; on top, every frame, a flat figure is drawn pixel by pixel at the scene's pixel size: a rounded head with the face, eyes and a touch of blush towards where they look (or the hair from behind; short hair stops above the ears, long hair falls behind), arms and legs as thick strokes with a darker underside, the body with light and shade, what they hold drawn flat in its colour (cup, phone, book, axe, guitar, rod, watering can, log, laptop), the umbrella as a striped cloth dome, with no dark outline; they are a little smaller than before (`S` 1.05) and take the light of the hour. The fruit and flowers on the tree crowns also clear when the crown does. **Fewer blocks:** the beehives are rounded straw skeps made of clusters, the sunflowers have petals and seeds made of coloured dots, the chopping stump is round with its rings, the office chairs are wooden garden chairs with slats, and lumps of earth and roots hang under every step of the island so its lower edge is not a straight box. **Phase 3 (Nov 2026): life.** *Icons over the head* like Stardew Valley's (`EMOTES`, `EMOTE_OF`, `drawEmote`): a 7 × 7 pixel icon (music note, heart, alert, Zzz, sweat drop, thinking dots, light bulb, smile, sparkle, question mark) in a small white bubble with a tail, drawn on each person's own canvas above the head (or above the umbrella), popping in with a little hop and lasting about 2.4 s. They come from events (a fish bites, the bees attack, someone trips, a jog ends, petting the cat, greeting) and, now and then, from what they are doing (music while playing the guitar, thinking dots or an idea at the desk, Zzz in the hammock, sparkles at the lookout...), spaced 8–20 s apart per person. *Together:* breakfast first thing after waking up (coffee, if it is before 11:30 and not raining), and coffee, the picnic and the campfire are much more tempting when someone is already there, so they eat and gather together. *Greeting:* when two people pass each other (within 1.4 m, at most every 90 s per pair) they wave and show a smile or a heart. *Animation:* they speed up when they set off and slow down in the last steps (the walking cycle follows the speed), the head turns towards a new direction before the body, and when still they breathe and now and then look around. **Phase 4 (Nov 2026): a bigger island with new zones.** The island grew from 14 × 14 to 19 × 19 m (`H` 9.5; the rock steps underneath grow with it); the hammock islet moved out to make room and full screen shows everything a little smaller. New zones, low things in front and tall things at the back so nothing hides what happens: **long table and wood-fired oven** in front (a table with a cloth, bread and plates and two benches for six; at lunch, 12:48–14:48, and dinner, 20:12–21:48, eating together is by far the most tempting thing to do; someone bakes before, and the oven mouth glows while baking and in the evening; the oven dome is made of clay clusters); **henhouse** front left (a coop with a ramp, a low fence with its gate, grain on the ground and four hens made of clusters that wander and peck, and go to the coop at night; people feed them from outside, mostly in the morning); **clothesline** front right (seven coloured clothes that sway with the wind, more with clouds and rain; laundry in the morning); **glass greenhouse** on the right (wooden frame, very light glass, two beds of tomato plants and flowers; you go in through the left; it also shelters from the rain); **workshop** on the back of the terrace (a little tiled roof on four posts, a workbench with tools and fresh pots, and a potter's wheel with a stool that spins while someone works it); **high islet with an observatory** behind the house, higher than the island and reached by a ramp of planks with handrails (a round whitewashed tower with a dome that turns slowly at night and a red telescope on a tripod outside for watching the stars at night). New paths lead to each zone; every place is reachable on foot and nobody climbs where they should not (checked with the simulation). **The islet (reworked):** its grass top is flat with a soft mound (the rim used to take the height of the big island and rose in green spikes); the hammock hangs sideways to the camera between two trees with high crowns (it used to be seen end-on, like a tower of stripes); the swing hangs from the oak at the back, in front of it; underneath, a shorter rock with lumps of earth and roots hanging like the big island (no longer a pointed heart). **Fixes (Nov 2026):** the house roof rests on the walls and the gables (it used to float about 30 cm above them); the island block had a lid at ground level that covered the pond and the stream, so the water read as lawn (now the water is visible); three trees moved so they no longer hide the rope bridge, the lookout and the pond (the terrace cherry to the hives corner, the pine by the rope bridge further along the edge, and the oak by the ducks replaced by two low bushes). At night the pills turn cream with the chosen one in tomato red. The page background is set by the scene (pink by day, tinted at dawn and dusk, dark at night). It only draws while the tab is visible. Details of the scene itself (written when it lived on About; where it says «card» it no longer exists, only the full-screen version is used):
- **The island (what it shows):** a floating isometric pixel-art island in the spirit of x.com/daniildesigns' farm game: volume and several levels (cliffs with soil and rock strata, hanging rock blocks below, a raised terrace with a wooden fence and stone stairs), a cottage (lime plaster, timber frame, tiled roof, chimney with smoke, window boxes, porch bench, firewood), a woodshed with a chopping block, two raised vegetable beds and a water barrel, a pond with two ducks and shore stones that flows down a stream under a wooden bridge and falls off the cliff as a waterfall, beehives and a lookout bench on the terrace, a campfire with log seats, two street lamps, a painter's easel whose canvas fills with brushstrokes, a picnic blanket with a basket, and **a second floating islet** on the left (round rock with strata and a cone underneath) reached by a sagging rope bridge, with a striped hammock between two trees and a rope swing, the outdoor office (two desks, whiteboard, coffee table, bench), pixel-art vegetation that sways (oaks, some with apples, pink cherry trees in blossom with petals on the ground, small autumn trees, tiered pines and a weeping willow over the pond; flowering bushes, ferns at the tree bases, lavender, thousands of grass tufts and wildflowers), climbing vines on the cottage with wisteria hanging from the eaves, sunflowers, lily pads, pots on the porch, a string of coloured fairy lights from the house to a post by the campfire, the cherry tree and the whiteboard (they glow at night) and bunting on the terrace fence, rocks, mushrooms, hundreds of flowers and an orange cat. Three people (each with a strong colour, their own clothes, hair and umbrella; jointed at hips, knees, shoulders, elbows, waist and neck) live there and choose what to do from their needs (energy, social, focus, fun, nature, food), their character (red: work, coffee and wood; yellow: garden, bees, ducks and views; white: chats, campfire, stars, fishing and the cat, and the night owl), the hour and the weather, and they avoid what they just did, so it never repeats: type at the desks, explain at the whiteboard, coffee, read or check the phone on a bench, sit, stretch or do yoga on a hill, water or weed the garden, chop wood and carry a log to the house, watch the bees, fish from the bridge or the bank (with bites and ripples), feed the ducks, enjoy the view from the terrace or the cliff edge, rest against a tree, phone calls, chat (with a wave), pet the cat, sit at the campfire roasting marshmallows, lie on the terrace looking at the stars, sit on the porch, shelter under the shed, or go inside; and, depending on the hour: jog a lap of the path in the morning (then catch their breath), juggle, paint at the easel, have a picnic at midday, nap in the hammock, swing, fly a kite from a hill, play the guitar at the campfire, on the porch or on a bench (music notes float up and others come to dance), jump in puddles or dance in the rain. Funny bits: now and then someone trips and falls flat before getting up, the bees sometimes get angry and chase the beekeeper across the island, and at night the one who cannot sleep may come out sleepwalking with arms stretched forward. When it starts to rain someone goes to the desks, closes the laptops and carries them into the house; whoever next goes to work brings one back out. At night they go home one by one and sleep (lit windows, then Zzz from the window); now and then one cannot sleep and comes out for a while. In the rain they open umbrellas, stop outdoor work and shelter. Time follows Madrid (today's sunrise and sunset): the sun crosses the sky with long shadows at dawn and dusk; dusk is warm, night is moonlit with lamps, fireflies and a lit campfire. Weather: clear, cloudy (puffy pixel clouds drifting across the top of the picture, at most four at once, casting shadows) or gentle rain (rain streaks, ripples, wetter ground), and by default it changes by itself every few minutes. On the card the island (with its islet) is drawn smaller and centred, like the emblem in the middle of a card back; full screen it fits the whole width of the island plus the islet (so on a phone held upright both are seen, island smaller and centred; you zoom in from there) and fills the window on wider screens. The background is set by the scene: white on the card and pink (#ffa2d4) full screen by day, tinted at dawn and dusk, greyer with clouds, dark at night. It is a real three.js scene (js/about-scene.js) rendered at low resolution and enlarged without smoothing, with **one coherent pixel grain**: each scene pixel covers a whole number of real device pixels (about 380 scene pixels wide on the card, 900 full screen), so every pixel is the same size and nothing shimmers; every texture has the same density (32 texels per metre), light falls in four flat bands (toon shading, no gradients), and foliage, grass tufts and clouds are made of hundreds of hand-drawn 8 × 8 leaf/tuft/puff clusters (several variants, randomly flipped, five tones picked by the sun direction plus highlight and gap pixels) that always face the camera and keep the same pixel size; invisible spheres cast their shadows. No low-poly facets and no cube volumes; hard objects follow the same organic style: hand-painted textures (irregular roof tiles with moss, lime plaster with blotches and cracks, rounded stones), umbrellas as cloth domes, boulders and pond stones as rounded clusters, a grass fringe hanging over every edge and moss on roofs and steps. Flowers, fruit, petals and bulbs are 2-pixel dots. Cliffs do not receive shadows. Everything is drawn in code (no external images), static parts are merged into a few meshes, shadows are 1024 px on the card and 2048 full screen. It is built before the flip (when the mouse first comes near the photo, or in idle time on touch screens once the photo is on screen), its textures are painted in memory and its shaders compiled in the background, so the flip stays smooth; only animates while it is turned and on screen, and stays still with reduced motion. The whole card tilts with the holographic foil as before; full screen it stays flat. On the turned card (desktop and phone alike), clicking the island makes the card grow to fill the window (0.5 s, ease-in-out; page scroll locked); clicking outside the island brings it back, and clicking outside the island on the card turns it back to the photo. **Full screen:** the controls follow the site's system (no boxes, shadows, gradients or blur, no help text): the tomato stays at the top left and a «Close» word sits where «Menu» is (24 px gutter, 64 px bar, 14 px Funnel Sans); bottom left, two rows of 999 px pills: **Morning · Day · Sunset · Night · Now** (time of day; «Now» follows the real hour in Madrid, and choosing a time turns it off) and **Clear · Cloudy · Rain · Auto** (weather; «Auto» lets it change by itself, and choosing one turns it off). Pills are white with ink text like the site's buttons, the chosen one is ink with white text; at night they turn cream and the chosen one tomato red (`--menu-red`, cream on it 4.5:1). Bottom right, two round buttons zoom out and in (hidden on phones, where you pinch). They rise in one after another once the card has grown (0.45 s, `--ease-out`; none with reduced motion), 36 px tall (40 px on touch), a quick scale on press, focus ring in ink (cream at night); the groups are named for screen readers with `aria-label` and the pressed state is `aria-pressed`. The wheel or a pinch zooms towards the pointer in whole-pixel steps (each step adds one device pixel per scene pixel, up to about ×3: the pixels grow crisply, like enlarging a pixel-art drawing, never stretched halfway), dragging pans (kept within the island, snapped to whole pixels), a double click or double tap zooms in or back out; keyboard: + and − zoom, arrows pan, Escape steps back. The cursor pill reads «Flip», «Open», «Zoom» or «Drag» over the island, and «Close» outside it.
