# Design detail: Digital Archive and its collections

Detail for this area of the design system. `DESIGN.md` holds the system and a summary; read this file only when working on this area, and keep new per-page detail here, not in `DESIGN.md`.

- **Digital Archive (/archive/, /archive/fruit-stickers/):** page black with cream text, like the Pokémon album. The index has the same structure as «Selected projects» on the landing page: two columns, a third and two thirds, with the title «Digital Archive» on the left (up to 100 px, `clamp(40px, 5.2vw, 100px)`, sticky under the bar) with its 20 px credit under it («Inspired by my friend Nicolò Lombardi’s digital archive», with a link to nicolo-lombardi.com/archive/ that opens in a new tab; 14 px on a phone), and on the right one row per collection, as a block centred in the right-hand space from 761 px up, both horizontally and vertically (mid-height of the screen, the same space above and below), well apart from the title, with the names flush left (ragged right: they all start at the same left edge); the title stays at the top left; on a phone they stay left-aligned under the title (stacked under the title below 760 px, title up to 96 px) (name at 22–40 px, `clamp(22px, 2.4vw, 40px)`, well below the title so that the title stands out, with its count; no thumbnails; no horizontal lines between the rows: they are separated by space, 12–24 px above and below each name; the black part always fills the screen (`min-height: 100svh`), so the red footer is only seen when you scroll down, on both this index and the Playground menu). A collection is an endless wall in the same skewed lattice as the Pokémon album (same engine, square pieces at 1.1× size), with no captions or any text: the name of each piece lives only in its alt text. Pressing a piece (or Enter) opens its viewer, as in the reference: a black screen with the name huge at the top (words rise out of their masks), the piece at the centre and its facts at the bottom as plain columns with no lines (small label above, the value below in 20–40 px Funnel Display 600, all flush left across the full width; two columns on a phone; shown only when the piece has them), with «Close» at the top right (also Esc). The piece arrives with a bright flash that fades (1.1 s) and tilts up to 20° towards the pointer wherever it is on the whole screen, not only over the piece, with a soft highlight following it (on touch, while dragging); reduced motion removes the tilt, the flash and the rises. The address carries the piece (`#sticker-03`), so it can be shared and Back closes the viewer. A black gradient under the fixed bar keeps it legible while pieces pass beneath. In the menu, «Digital Archive» sits below the five big links as a smaller line (24 px on a phone, last in the column). The pieces are scans of real stickers cropped to their shape (Pink Lady, Plátano de Canarias so far), each at its own proportions inside a square cell.
- **Fave album covers (/archive/fave-album-covers/):** second collection, same structure as Fruit stickers (square covers instead of cut-out stickers; the viewer lists Artist, Album, Year and Designer). Eleven covers so far (Lost Weekend, Rubber Soul, Sgt. Pepper, Beatles 1, Ram, Romance, Dopamine Chamber, McCartney, Ambient 1, Music for Nine Post Cards, Minecraft – Volume Alpha), in the viewer from the files Mario supplied, converted to WebP (long side up to 2000 px; sizes range from 500 px for McCartney to 2000 px for Sgt. Pepper and Dopamine Chamber; the smaller ones (McCartney 500, Lost Weekend 700, Beatles 1 721) can be replaced with larger files in `assets/archive/fave-album-covers/` when available). In the viewer the piece is sized against the space left under the title, so long titles never overlap the facts; titles over 20 characters are set smaller (max 88 px) and over 30 smaller still (max 64 px). Mario gives the cover; the album, artist, year and designer facts are looked up from public sources, and a missing credit shows «—» (edit `items.json`).
- **Pixel trail (Digital Archive: index and both walls, in white; Posters, in dark ink `#0c0c0b` on its light page, set with `data-trail="ink"` on `<body>`; the Posters page no longer has the dot-matrix background):** a trail of white round dots on a 340-column grid (finer dots, about 4 px cells at 1440 px) follows the mouse (React Bits «PixelTrail», ported to plain JavaScript in js/pixel-trail.js; mounted by js/archive-trail.js). Dots are tiny, about 2–3 px (gap 0.5 of a cell), the brush is narrow (0.075 of the long side) and they drop out in a dither pattern over 0.8 s, with no shine or glow and no click burst; one fixed full-screen canvas BEHIND the content (z-index -1: the trail passes under the pieces, covers and texts, never over them; `<html>` paints the page colour and `<body>` is transparent so it does not hide the trail), never catching clicks. Mouse only: not created on touch, with reduced motion, with data saver or without WebGL2 (then the page is as before). The values live at the top of js/archive-trail.js.
- **Back button (collection pages):** «Back» with a small left arrow sits in the fixed bar just right of the tomato (14 px, white at 70 %, white on hover with the arrow nudging 3 px left; it takes the bar's difference blend) and links to /archive/ (a real link, aria-label «Back to Digital Archive»). The piece viewer keeps its own «Close» and covers the bar.
