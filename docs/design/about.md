# Design detail: About: layout and the back of the portrait (flip scene)

Detail for this area of the design system. `DESIGN.md` holds the system and a summary; read this file only when working on this area, and keep new per-page detail here, not in `DESIGN.md`.

- **About (restored to the 28 Oct design, «foto grande y presentación a dos columnas»):** green page with lime text. On desktop the holographic portrait stays still on the left (sticky, up to 40 vw wide, 4:5, it still tilts to the mouse with a moving shadow) and the text card sits on the right (from 46 vw) and scrolls with the page: the huge «Mario Diz» title (Funnel Display), the biography in bold across both columns, two columns below (Art direction & visual design · Values), «Now playing» with the two covers, LinkedIn and the email under the two columns, Selected clients (the logos) and the FAQs link; then the big footer. The card enters from the right on a direct load (0.8 s) and each block fades up as it appears. On a phone the photo stays on top (sticky) and the card passes over it. The email shown is the current one (contact@dizmar.xyz, not the old Gmail one of that version) and the breadcrumbs and the small footer of that version are not back (the big footer replaces it). The images keep the mosaic load (js/pixel-load.js, `.px`; it measures the image through an ImageBitmap because with srcset + sizes `naturalWidth` is density-corrected and the crop came out magnified): each starts as an embedded 10-pixel-wide mosaic and sharpens in six steps when it arrives and is in view; `<html>` gets `fx` only when IntersectionObserver exists and reduced motion is not requested. The Locomotive-style version (4 Nov, PR #348) is in the history if it is ever wanted back.
- **About · the back of the portrait:** clicking (or Enter/Space on) the photo turns it over in 0.9 s (rotateY, ease-in-out; instant with reduced motion) and shows a small isometric pixel-art scene in the style of shawn.'s «Small plays – Basketball» (x.com/zzzzshawn): a white card with a floating lawn island with small hills, whose edge frays into loose pixels, a dirt path around it and mown stripes inside; on it three people (each with a strong colour and their own clothes and hair: short or long sleeves, trousers or shorts, short or long hair or a cap; jointed at hips, knees, shoulders, elbows, waist and neck, with eyes) work outdoors and decide on their own what to do next: type at one of the two desks (now and then leaning back with their hands behind the head), explain at the whiteboard taking turns, get a coffee and sip it, sit on the bench looking at the phone, sit on a hilltop or stretch there, take a phone call, or walk over to someone and chat with their hands. They find their way around the furniture (a grid path search) and it never repeats the same way; long hard shadows fall towards the lower right. It is a real three.js scene (js/about-scene.js) rendered at low resolution (each scene pixel is 1.5 to 3 screen pixels: about 260 wide on the card, sharper full screen) and enlarged without smoothing; the background is transparent and set by CSS; it is drawn in code (no external images), loads only on the first flip (prefetched on hover), only animates while it is turned and on screen, and stays still with reduced motion. The whole card tilts (a `.tilt` wrapper around both faces carries --rx/--ry and the shine variables), so the back behaves like the photo: it leans towards the mouse (or sways by itself on touch screens), its shadow moves and a holographic foil runs across it (the same rainbow bands, multiplied over the white so they show, plus the light glare); full screen the card stays flat and without shine. On the turned card (desktop and phone alike), clicking the island makes the card grow to fill the window on pink (#ffa2d4, 0.5 s, ease-in-out; page scroll locked); clicking outside the island brings it back to the card, and clicking outside the island on the card turns it back to the photo. Keyboard: Enter turns the photo, Enter on the back opens full screen, Escape steps back. The cursor pill reads «Flip», «Open» or «Close».
