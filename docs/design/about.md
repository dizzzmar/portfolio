- **About (restored to the 28 Oct design, «foto grande y presentación a dos columnas»):** green page with lime text. On desktop the holographic portrait stays still on the left (sticky, up to 40 vw wide, 4:5, it still tilts to the mouse with a moving shadow) and the text card sits on the right (from 46 vw) and scrolls with the page: the huge «Mario Diz» title (Funnel Display), the biography in bold across both columns, two columns below (Art direction & visual design · Values), «Now playing» with the two covers, LinkedIn and the email under the two columns, Selected clients (the logos) and the FAQs link; then the big footer. The images keep the mosaic load (js/pixel-load.js, `.px`; it measures the image through an ImageBitmap because with srcset + sizes `naturalWidth` is density-corrected and the crop came out magnified): each starts as an embedded 10-pixel-wide mosaic and sharpens in six steps when it arrives and is in view; `<html>` gets `fx` only when IntersectionObserver exists and reduced motion is not requested. The Locomotive-style version (4 Nov, PR #348) is in the history if it is ever wanted back.
- **About · the back of the portrait (a small living world):** clicking (or Enter/Space on) the photo turns it over in 0.9 s (rotateY, ease-in-out; instant with reduced motion) and shows a floating isometric pixel-art island in the spirit of x.com/daniildesigns' farm game: volume and several levels (cliffs with soil and rock strata, hanging rock blocks below, a raised terrace with a wooden fence and stone stairs), a cottage (lime plaster, timber frame, tiled roof, chimney with smoke, window boxes, porch bench, firewood), a woodshed with a chopping block, two raised vegetable beds and a water barrel, a pond with two ducks and shore stones that flows down a stream under a wooden bridge and falls off the cliff as a waterfall, beehives and a lookout bench on the terrace, a campfire with log seats, two street lamps, a painter's easel whose canvas fills with brushstrokes, a picnic blanket with a basket, and **a second floating islet** on the left (round rock with strata and a cone underneath) reached by a sagging rope bridge, with a striped hammock between two trees and a rope swing, the outdoor office (two desks, whiteboard, coffee table, bench), pixel-art vegetation that sways (oaks, some with apples, pink cherry trees in blossom with petals on the ground, small autumn trees, tiered pines and a weeping willow over the pond; flowering bushes, ferns at the tree bases, lavender, thousands of grass tufts and wildflowers), climbing vines on the cottage with wisteria hanging from the eaves, sunflowers, lily pads, pots on the porch, a string of coloured fairy lights from the house to a post by the campfire, the cherry tree and the whiteboard (they glow at night) and bunting on the terrace fence, rocks, mushrooms, hundreds of flowers and an orange cat. Three people (each with a strong colour, their own clothes, hair and umbrella; jointed at hips, knees, shoulders, elbows, waist and neck) live there and choose what to do from their needs (energy, social, focus, fun, nature, food), their character (red: work, coffee and wood; yellow: garden, bees, ducks and views; white: chats, campfire, stars, fishing and the cat, and the night owl), the hour and the weather, and they avoid what they just did, so it never repeats: type at the desks, explain at the whiteboard, coffee, read or check the phone on a bench, sit, stretch or do yoga on a hill, water or weed the garden, chop wood and carry a log to the house, watch the bees, fish from the bridge or the bank (with bites and ripples), feed the ducks, enjoy the view from the terrace or the cliff edge, rest against a tree, phone calls, chat (with a wave), pet the cat, sit at the campfire roasting marshmallows, lie on the terrace looking at the stars, sit on the porch, shelter under the shed, or go inside; and, depending on the hour: jog a lap of the path in the morning (then catch their breath), juggle, paint at the easel, have a picnic at midday, nap in the hammock, swing, fly a kite from a hill, play the guitar at the campfire, on the porch or on a bench (music notes float up and others come to dance), jump in puddles or dance in the rain. Funny bits: now and then someone trips and falls flat before getting up, the bees sometimes get angry and chase the beekeeper across the island, and at night the one who cannot sleep may come out sleepwalking with arms stretched forward. When it starts to rain someone goes to the desks, closes the laptops and carries them into the house; whoever next goes to work brings one back out. At night they go home one by one and sleep (lit windows, then Zzz from the window); now and then one cannot sleep and comes out for a while. In the rain they open umbrellas, stop outdoor work and shelter. Time follows Madrid (today's sunrise and sunset): the sun crosses the sky with long shadows at dawn and dusk; dusk is warm, night is moonlit with lamps, fireflies and a lit campfire. Weather: clear, cloudy (puffy pixel clouds drifting across the top of the picture, at most four at once, casting shadows) or gentle rain (rain streaks, ripples, wetter ground), and by default it changes by itself every few minutes. On the card the island (with its islet) is drawn smaller and centred, like the emblem in the middle of a card back; full screen it fits the whole width of the island plus the islet (so on a phone held upright both are seen, island smaller and centred; you zoom in from there) and fills the window on wider screens. The background is set by the scene: white on the card and pink (#ffa2d4) full screen by day, tinted at dawn and dusk, greyer with clouds, dark at night. It is a real three.js scene (js/about-scene.js) rendered at low resolution and enlarged without smoothing, with **one coherent pixel grain**: each scene pixel covers a whole number of real device pixels (about 380 scene pixels wide on the card, 900 full screen), so every pixel is the same size and nothing shimmers; every texture has the same density (32 texels per metre), light falls in four flat bands (toon shading, no gradients), and foliage, grass tufts and clouds are made of hundreds of hand-drawn 8 × 8 leaf/tuft/puff clusters (several variants, randomly flipped, five tones picked by the sun direction plus highlight and gap pixels) that always face the camera and keep the same pixel size; invisible spheres cast their shadows. No low-poly facets and no cube volumes; hard objects follow the same organic style: hand-painted textures (irregular roof tiles with moss, lime plaster with blotches and cracks, rounded stones), umbrellas as cloth domes, boulders and pond stones as rounded clusters, a grass fringe hanging over every edge and moss on roofs and steps. Flowers, fruit, petals and bulbs are 2-pixel dots. Cliffs do not receive shadows. Everything is drawn in code (no external images), static parts are merged into a few meshes, shadows are 1024 px on the card and 2048 full screen. It is built before the flip (when the mouse first comes near the photo, or in idle time on touch screens once the photo is on screen), its textures are painted in memory and its shaders compiled in the background, so the flip stays smooth; only animates while it is turned and on screen, and stays still with reduced motion. The whole card tilts with the holographic foil as before; full screen it stays flat. On the turned card (desktop and phone alike), clicking the island makes the card grow to fill the window (0.5 s, ease-in-out; page scroll locked); clicking outside the island brings it back, and clicking outside the island on the card turns it back to the photo. **Full screen:** the controls follow the site's system (no boxes, shadows, gradients or blur, no help text): the tomato stays at the top left and a «Close» word sits where «Menu» is (24 px gutter, 64 px bar, 14 px Funnel Sans); bottom left, two rows of 999 px pills: **Morning · Day · Sunset · Night · Now** (time of day; «Now» follows the real hour in Madrid, and choosing a time turns it off) and **Clear · Cloudy · Rain · Auto** (weather; «Auto» lets it change by itself, and choosing one turns it off). Pills are white with ink text like the site's buttons, the chosen one is ink with white text; at night they turn cream and the chosen one tomato red (`--menu-red`, cream on it 4.5:1). Bottom right, two round buttons zoom out and in (hidden on phones, where you pinch). They rise in one after another once the card has grown (0.45 s, `--ease-out`; none with reduced motion), 36 px tall (40 px on touch), a quick scale on press, focus ring in ink (cream at night); the groups are named for screen readers with `aria-label` and the pressed state is `aria-pressed`. The wheel or a pinch zooms towards the pointer in whole-pixel steps (each step adds one device pixel per scene pixel, up to about ×3: the pixels grow crisply, like enlarging a pixel-art drawing, never stretched halfway), dragging pans (kept within the island, snapped to whole pixels), a double click or double tap zooms in or back out; keyboard: + and − zoom, arrows pan, Escape steps back. The cursor pill reads «Flip», «Open», «Zoom» or «Drag» over the island, and «Close» outside it.
